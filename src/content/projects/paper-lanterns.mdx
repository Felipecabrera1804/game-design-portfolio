## The premise

*This is placeholder text showing what a finished case study looks like. Replace it with your own project.*

The jam theme was "only one". We built a short game about writing the last letter to someone you love, where the ink in your pen is the only resource.

## Why this mechanic

Our first version used a timer. Playtesters rushed, and the urgency read as stress instead of tenderness. Ink kept the scarcity and took away the panic, because the player can think for as long as they want. They just can't *say* it all.

<Callout kind="insight">
Scarcity of *time* made players anxious; scarcity of *words* made them careful. Careful was the feeling we wanted.
</Callout>

## Process

1. Paper prototype with physical word cards and a token budget.
2. Ink script with weighted phrases: short words are cheap, emotional words are expensive.
3. Added the desk of unsent drafts after a tester said they wanted to "keep" the words they deleted.

<Callout kind="failed">
Letting players refill ink by finding bottles in the room broke the theme. It turned grief into a scavenger hunt, so we cut it.
</Callout>

## Reflection

- **What worked:** players spent minutes on a single sentence, with no tutorial telling them to.
- **What I'd change:** the ending tells you how the letter was received; I'd leave it ambiguous.
- **What I'd take forward:** constrain the *expression*, not the *thinking*.
